More detail · what each indicator measures

Methods and sources

Sources

Day-ahead prices (updated daily), generation by source, demand and cross-border physical flows: ENTSO-E Transparency Platform. Household prices: Eurostat (nrg_pc_204, nrg_pc_202), half-yearly, all taxes included. Pump prices: European Commission Weekly Oil Bulletin. TTF, storage and the weekly indicators: DG ENER weekly newsletter. Country borders on the map: Natural Earth.

Prices

Daily averages are weighted by interval length (hourly until September 2025, 15-minute after). Days and months follow Brussels time. Country price for countries with several zones (Italy, Denmark, Sweden): simple mean of the zones, not the PUN. EU27 price: country prices weighted by monthly demand; by hour, weighted by each country's demand in that hour. Cyprus and Malta have no coupled day-ahead market.

Prices through the day: each point is the average of that hour over all days of the period. Solar pushes prices down around midday; when the sun sets, demand is still high and the price rises. "% of day average" removes the price level (gas, CO2), so only the shape of the day is compared. Storage, demand response, interconnections and the mix all affect the shape: these data alone cannot separate their effects.

Generation mix and demand

ENTSO-E actual generation per production type and actual load. Some countries report with gaps; Malta does not report generation by source. The running month is left out of the mix and demand.

Through the day: what covers demand

The chart is an hourly balance. Above zero: everything that enters the system in that hour, that is generation by source plus imports. Below zero: what is not consumed in the country in that hour: exports, power charging batteries and power pumping water into reservoirs. Above minus below equals demand, so the part above zero passes 100% by as much as the part below zero goes under zero. Imports and exports are gross, summed over all borders: a country can import on one border and export on another in the same hour, for example when power transits through it.

The balance never closes exactly, for three reasons: measured demand (ENTSO-E "actual load") does not always cover self-consumption and small plants; grid losses; and gaps in the data for some sources, borders or hours. The gap is a few points for most countries and larger for some, Germany included.

Batteries are what ENTSO-E reports as "Energy storage", available from 2025 and only for some countries; pumped hydro is shown separately. Many countries, Germany included, do not report batteries: a missing band does not mean there are no batteries. The EU27 battery figure is the sum of the reporting countries and is understated. For the EU27, imports and exports are with non-EU countries only. Winter runs from December of the year before to February.

Cross-border flows

Physical flows are what actually crossed the border, including power in transit and loop flows; they differ from the commercial exchanges agreed in the market. Daily totals in Brussels time. Days with more than four missing hours are left out, so totals for periods with gaps are understated: the table under "More detail" shows the days with data. Hour-by-hour data for single days are kept for the last 35 days.

Compare countries

Each axis is the country value divided by the value of the comparison (the EU27 average or another country). The grey shape is the comparison (1×). Values above 2.5× are capped (▲). Indicators based on imports and exports use the simple mean of member states for the EU27, because flows between EU countries cancel out in an EU total.
